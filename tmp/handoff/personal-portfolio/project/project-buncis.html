The problem

Why it needed building

The subsidiary tracked assets across spreadsheets and disconnected tools, so reconciling what existed, where it was, and who was responsible took days and was error-prone. They needed a single source of truth that their existing Odoo ERP could grow into — not another silo.

My role

What I contributed

I worked across the stack inside Odoo: modelling the inventory and asset domain in Python, wiring the ORM relations and access rules, and building the QWeb report templates the operations team actually prints. I also reviewed teammates' pull requests and wrote test cases so changes shipped with confidence.

The build

What I built

  • Custom Odoo modules for asset registration, movement, and audit history.
  • Server-side business logic in Python with validated state transitions.
  • Printable QWeb/PDF reports for stock-takes and management review.
  • Role-based permissions so field and office staff saw only what they needed.
Outcome

Impact

Working inside a mature ERP taught me to design with the grain of an existing system rather than against it — extending Odoo's models and respecting its conventions made the work maintainable for the team that inherited it. It also sharpened my instinct for data integrity: in operations software, a wrong number is worse than a missing feature.
